# Pitch Brief, Claim Ledger, and Review

Use this reference to prepare or review a product or system presentation speech.

## Brief Template

```text
Product or system:
Authorized sources:
Audience:
Audience awareness and objections:
Occasion:
Desired decision or action:
Speaking duration:
Q&A or demo time:
Language and voice:
Approved proof:
Restricted claims or disclosures:
Required points:
Excluded topics:
Assumptions requiring confirmation:
```

If the source is a repository, inspect the smallest useful evidence set:

- README and user-facing documentation;
- runnable entry points and primary workflows;
- configuration and supported integrations;
- tests that demonstrate material behavior;
- screenshots, demos, releases, or metrics supplied by the user;
- limitations, feature flags, incomplete paths, and compatibility boundaries.

Treat implementation evidence as proof of behavior only to the extent actually demonstrated. A code path does not prove adoption, usability, performance, security, savings, or customer outcomes.

## Claim Authority

Classify each potential claim:

| Class | Meaning | Allowed treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Directly supported by supplied implementation, documentation, test, demo, or measurement | State within the demonstrated scope |
| Approved | Explicitly supplied and approved by the product owner | State with supplied qualifications |
| Inferred | Reasonable interpretation not directly demonstrated | Label as inference or convert into a question |
| External | Depends on current market, competitor, legal, or category evidence | Research and cite before use |
| Unsupported | No adequate source or conflicts with available evidence | Exclude from the speech |

Pay extra attention to:

- fastest, best, first, only, leading, revolutionary, effortless, secure, compliant, and guaranteed;
- percentages, time saved, money saved, accuracy, uptime, scale, and performance;
- customer names, testimonials, adoption, awards, certifications, partnerships, and roadmap dates;
- compatibility and availability across platforms, plans, regions, and versions.

## Selling-Point Matrix

Use one row per candidate selling point:

| Capability | Evidence | Audience problem or job | Mechanism | Benefit | Differentiator | Proof | Limitation | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rank candidates using:

1. audience relevance;
2. evidence strength;
3. distinctness from the other points;
4. importance to the requested decision;
5. ease of explaining and demonstrating within the time limit.

Select three to five primary points. Move secondary features into examples, supporting proof, Q&A, or an appendix rather than weakening the main narrative.

## Occasion Patterns

### Sales or Customer Demo

Use:

1. recognizable customer problem;
2. consequence of the current approach;
3. product promise;
4. three selling points with proof;
5. focused demonstration;
6. relevant objection or limitation;
7. next step.

Keep the demonstration tied to a decision criterion. Prepare a verbal fallback when the live environment fails.

### Launch or Keynote

Use:

1. meaningful change in the audience's world;
2. why the old approach is no longer enough;
3. product reveal and controlling idea;
4. capability progression through concrete scenarios;
5. proof and availability boundary;
6. memorable close and action.

Do not manufacture novelty, secrecy, or urgency.

### Executive or Investor Update

Use:

1. business problem and stakes;
2. product thesis;
3. evidence of capability or progress;
4. strategic advantage and constraints;
5. risks, next milestone, and requested decision.

Separate product evidence from forecasts, market assumptions, and financial claims.

### Technical Talk

Use:

1. problem and operating constraints;
2. rejected or competing approaches;
3. architecture or design decisions;
4. demonstrated behavior and tradeoffs;
5. lessons, limitations, and reusable insight.

Do not present implementation complexity as innovation without explaining its effect.

### Internal Adoption

Use:

1. current workflow friction;
2. what changes and what remains stable;
3. role-specific benefits;
4. evidence and realistic limitations;
5. adoption steps, support, and feedback path.

Avoid implying that all teams share the same problem or readiness.

## Timing

Allocate time before drafting. A useful starting range is:

| Section | Share of speaking time |
| --- | ---: |
| Hook and context | 5-10% |
| Problem and stakes | 10-15% |
| Product promise | 10-15% |
| Selling points and proof | 35-45% |
| Demo, objection, or limitation | 10-20% |
| Close and call to action | 5-10% |

Adjust for the occasion and reserve any required demo or Q&A time explicitly. Validate duration with a realistic read-through in the delivery language. Word counts are only estimates because language, numbers, pauses, demonstrations, and speaker pace vary.

## Timed Outline

Use:

| Time | Section purpose | Spoken message | Evidence or example | Slide or demo cue | Transition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Make every section earn the next one. Remove repeated background, duplicated feature explanations, and slide narration before cutting proof or necessary caveats.

## Final Review Rubric

Score each dimension as `0 = missing`, `1 = partial`, or `2 = ready`:

- **Truth:** material claims map to authorized evidence.
- **Audience:** language, priorities, examples, and objections fit the listeners.
- **Value:** each primary feature becomes a concrete audience benefit.
- **Differentiation:** comparisons are relevant, specific, and supported.
- **Proof:** demonstrations, examples, or measurements support the promise.
- **Structure:** one controlling idea connects the opening, body, and close.
- **Speakability:** sentences, transitions, emphasis, and repetition work aloud.
- **Timing:** a realistic read-through fits the hard limit.
- **Delivery resilience:** slide, demo, and technical failures have a fallback where needed.
- **Action:** the audience knows the next authorized step.
- **Integrity:** limitations, uncertainty, confidentiality, and disclosures remain visible.

Do not call the pitch ready when `Truth`, `Timing`, or `Integrity` scores `0`.
